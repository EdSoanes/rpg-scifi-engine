System 10 · Core mechanics

Character

The six attributes, what they derive, and the skill groups they feed. Attributes never add to a roll.

The principle

Attributes never add to a task roll

The roll is 2d10 + skill rating, and nothing else. Attributes set the derived scores, give an untrained floor to the skills in their group, and make those skills cheaper to raise. A strong character hits harder, carries more and lasts longer. They do not hit more often.

Why attributes stay off the dice

The certainty rule and the 0 to 8 rating scale only work if the number on the roll is one number. Add an attribute to it and a Body 7 novice out-shoots a trained professional, the difficulty ladder drifts, and every table in these documents has to be recomputed with two inputs. Keeping attributes to derived scores gives a strong body its due in the places where strength shows: how hard the hit lands, how much is carried, how long the character stands.

  • Human range 2 to 5. Average 3. Augmentation takes an attribute to 8.
  • One scale on the sheet. An attribute of 4 and a skill of 4 mean the same thing: a professional's level.
  • The two pools come from two different attributes, so the samurai and the decker do not compete for one stat. Both still get most of their pool from augmentation.
  • Augmentation that raises an attribute raises everything derived from it. Wired reflexes that raise Reflexes raise Tempo, Move and the untrained Defence floor together. Gear that raises only Tempo is also possible and is the cheaper item.
Why six and these six

Fewer than six and the pools would share an attribute or a career would have no home. More and the differences stop meaning anything at the table. Awareness stands apart from Intellect because noticing and reasoning are different careers (the scout and the decker). Will stands apart from Presence because keeping your own nerve and moving someone else's are different careers (the rigger and the face).

Why the pools map in bands and not one to one

A pool of 5 from an unaugmented Reflexes 5 would make chrome optional for the fast character, and the gear page wants pools to come from augmentation. Banding keeps the human range at 2 to 4 and leaves the climb to 8 for the catalogue.

What the group does

  • Untrained floor. A skill at rating 0 is used at attribute − 3, minimum 0. Reflexes 5 shoots untrained at 2: has done it, no more. The floor can never exceed 5, and a trained rating always replaces it.
  • Raise cost. Raising a skill costs less when its group's attribute is higher. A character grows into their body without being locked out of anything.
  • Gates stay with the skill. A trained-only task needs the skill, not the attribute. Intellect 7 does not let you attempt surgery.
Why Rigging is under Will

Jacked-in control of drones and vehicles is sustained attention under pressure, not reasoning and not reflex. Putting it under Will gives the rigger a reason to want Focus and a group that is not the decker's. The rigger who also drives by hand has Drive under Reflexes for that.

Why Composure does two jobs

Composure is the Will group's core skill and the skill behind Social Defence. Keeping your nerve is one thing whether the pressure is a gun or a negotiation, and one skill for it means the face and the samurai are both buying the same thing when they decide not to be rattled.

Left out on purpose

  • No luck or edge pool. Pace and overdrive already give the player a risk dial. A third one would be spent on the same decisions.
  • No Reaction or Initiative attribute. Turn order follows pace. Nothing needs a stat.
  • No Humanity or Essence. What augmentation costs beyond money is a setting decision. The rules work with or without one.
  • No attribute rolls. A task that seems to need one (lift the gate, hold your breath) is an Athletics or Endurance task against a difficulty, with Body setting the effect.
Open: track sizes and the Move figure

How many boxes Health and Composure have per point of Body and Will, and how many metres a Tempo pip buys, are not set. They belong with the injury rules and the first fights.

Open: the skill list

Twenty skills is a first pass to show the groups work. Names, splits (is Firearms one skill or three?) and specialisations are decided with the gear catalogue, since the two have to agree.
