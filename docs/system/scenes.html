System 06 · Core mechanics

Scenes

Worked examples with real numbers: a sniper, a bomb, an archive, a matrix run. Checks that the rules produce the moments.

How to read these

  • Each scene states the character, the task and the numbers, then shows what the sheet would offer the player.
  • Single-roll chances are exact, from the distribution of the expression. Multi-roll outcomes (the bomb, the archive) are from simulation, two hundred thousand runs each, rounded.
  • The purpose is to check that the rules produce the intended moment: a real choice, with the trade-off visible. Where they do not, the rules change, not the scene.

The sniper Tempo

  • Character: rifles 6, smartlink +1, so rating 7. Wired: Tempo 6.
  • Target: a guard with defence 3, moving. Difficulty 11 + 3 = 14. Need 7.
  • Bad failure: the shot hits something it should not, or the muzzle flash gives the position away.
  • Methodical is not offered: the target can react.

  • Six Tempo buys three Normal shots, or a Careful shot and a Normal one, or two Normal shots with two dodges in hand.
  • Rushed has the best chance of an exceptional hit. That is the flat die doing its job: the snap shot is the one that produces the miracle, and the disaster.
  • A spotter with rifles 4 or better is qualified. With one, each Normal-cost shot rolls as Careful: three 90% shots a turn with no bad failure possible. The spotter spends 2 Focus a turn to do it, and does nothing else.
What this checks

Aiming has a cost in shots, not a bonus. Rushing is a real option, not a trap. The wired character's advantage is in volume and in having a reaction left, not in a better roll. The spotter changes the fight by giving up their own turn.

The device Focus

  • Character: demolitions 6. Focus 4.
  • Task: disarm a device. Difficulty 16, need 10. Extended, indivisible. Work total 5. Tolerance 1: the second bad failure detonates it. Base cost 2 Focus per roll.
  • Clock: six turns until it goes off anyway.
  • The rest of the team is holding a corridor. One of them has demolitions 4 and is qualified to help.

  • Alone, the choice is fast and 15% fatal, or safe and more likely than not to run out of time. Neither is good. That is the scene.
  • The helper resolves it, and the price is one less gun on the corridor. The team argues about that, not about the dice.
  • Methodical is certain and useless under a six-turn clock. It is the right answer when the device is not ticking.
  • With demolitions 5 instead of 6 the need becomes 11, and Careful no longer takes the bad failure off the table (a total of 11 against 16 is a bad failure). The sheet would say "Fair · can go badly" for every pace. That character should not be the one at the device.
What this checks

Time and safety trade against each other through pace alone. Cooperation is the lever that moves the trade-off, and it costs the team a body. A rating step changes the character of the scene rather than a percentage. Tolerance is what stops an extended task compounding a small risk into a certainty.

The archive Focus

  • Character: research 4. Outside turn tracking; Focus does not apply.
  • Task: find who really owns a shell company. Difficulty 14, need 10. Extended, divisible, interval one hour. Work total 6. Tolerance unlimited: a bad failure is a false lead and costs an hour and a point of progress.
  • A second character with research 3 is qualified to help.

  • Methodical is a promise: thirty hours and the answer is there, because 11 + 4 meets 14. If the difficulty were 16 the sheet would say "impossible" for Methodical, and the only way through would be to gamble on Normal or Careful rolls. A rating 4 researcher cannot grind out what a rating 6 researcher knows to look for.
  • "Can go badly" here is a false lead, not a disaster. The sheet uses the task's own word for it.
  • The GM ticks the hours. Each tick queues one progress roll per worker. The players settle them with real dice or the app, as usual.
What this checks

Non-combat work has the same shape as combat: a pace choice with time against risk, a margin that matters, cooperation that costs the helper their afternoon. Research that is beyond the researcher stays beyond them however long they spend; time buys reliability, not insight.

The matrix Focus Tempo

  • Decker: hacking 6, deck rating 5. Focus 6 from the deck, Tempo 2.
  • Samurai: hacking 2, borrowing a commlink. Focus 2, Tempo 6.
  • Node: rating 5. The deck meets the gate; the commlink is rating 2 and the node is three steps harder for it. Active defence, so difficulty 11 + 5 = 16 for the decker, 19 for the samurai (one step per rating short, then capped at Heroic). Methodical not offered.
  • Bad failure: the trace advances.

  • A guard rounds the corner and shoots at the decker: rifles 3 against the decker's static defence of 11 + 2 = 13. The guard hits 64% of the time. If the decker spends their 2 Tempo on a dodge, they roll 2d10 + 2 + 2 against the guard's 2d10 + 3 and the guard's chance falls to 47%. The decker then has nothing left for the node this turn.
  • The same guard shooting at the samurai (defence 5, static 16) hits 36%, and the samurai can dodge every shot and still attack twice.
What this checks

Two pools make two different characters fast in two different rooms, with the same rules. Gear gates: the deck is what makes the node attackable at the written difficulty. The decker's weakness is not a penalty; it is a small Tempo pool and the choice of spending it on a dodge or a hack.

What the scenes did not check

  • Damage, armour and injury. Not part of the core mechanics; they consume margin and effect values, which is all the core needs to provide.
  • Movement and range. Tempo costs for movement are open.
  • Long campaigns: whether four progression currencies keep pace with each other over twenty sessions. Only play will tell.
