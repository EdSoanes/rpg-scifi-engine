System 07 · Core mechanics

Engine

How each rule maps onto the engine, what belongs in the game system, and the few things the engine does not do yet.

The line between engine and system

Nothing here goes in the engine

Tempo, Focus, pace, margin and difficulty are game concepts. They are built in the game system assembly from the engine's properties, mods, states, actions and time. The engine gains only what any system could use.

  • The engine already provides: additive dice expressions with lowest, highest and average; pending and stored rolls; one-turn costs that return next turn; effects with lifespans; named time events the system declares; rules code that can request a roll on a time event; actions with cost and outcome steps; override mods; describe.
  • Every rule in these documents was checked against that list before it was written. The gaps are in the last section.

Mapping

Rule → engine construct
RuleBuilt as
RatingA whole-number property on the character per skill. Gear, specialisation and circumstance bonuses are mods on it, each with its origin, so describe shows where the number came from.
Task rollAn action whose roll step declares a whole-number input. The input's property holds the dice expression plus the rating; it is pending until rolled. The supplied number is the dice result, bonuses are added by the engine.
PaceA choice made when the action starts. It applies an override mod to the dice part of the roll property (1d20, 2d10, 1d10 + 5 or 11) and a multiplier to the cost step. Methodical leaves a constant, so no pending roll appears.
CertaintyCompare the roll property's lowest and highest against the difficulty before the roll step. Certain and impossible are reported by the action, never enforced. The step still runs if told to.
DifficultyA whole-number input of the action. Static: typed or chosen by word. From an opponent: 11 + rating, a calculated property on the opponent's sheet, read aloud or handed over.
Margin and effectThe outcome step reads the roll total and the difficulty, derives the band, and applies effect mods named for the band. Damage, heal and progress are mods with origins.
Tempo and FocusTwo whole-number properties. Each action cost is a one-turn negative mod in the cost step, so the pool returns next turn, exactly as Cyborgs focus does today. Outside turn tracking the engine skips the cost and records it as skipped.
Refund on marginA one-turn positive mod on the pool in the outcome step, named "time saved".
ReactionsAn action on the defender's sheet with a one-turn cost and its own roll. Its total is handed to the attacker, who types it as the difficulty. No connection between sheets is needed.
Multi-turn actionAn activity whose cost step is paid across turns: a running total of committed points on the action, and the roll step only becomes available when the total meets the cost. Reset of the step follows the engine's reset rules.
Extended taskA state on the character holding work total, progress and tolerance. On each interval event, or each turn tick in turn tracking, it requests a roll. Settling the roll applies progress. The state switches off when progress meets the total.
Interval eventsDeclared time events of the system: hour, day. The GM triggers them. Every sheet receives them, so every extended task on every sheet queues its roll.
OverdriveThe pool goes negative; the engine never refuses. A state "fatigued" switches on when the pool is below zero at end of turn and applies the next turn's penalty.
Damage and woundsA "take a hit" action on the defender's sheet with inputs for band, damage, type and location. Soak and armour are mods tagged by location. Wounds are states with an origin and a location. Bleeding is a recurring effect.
StunA negative mod on Tempo or Focus with a lifespan: one turn, several turns, or ending when time passes. "Knocked out" is a state that is on while the stunned pips meet the pool.
Restricted pointsA mod on the pool carrying a condition naming the action kind it pays for. The cost step spends conditional mods first.
HelpOn the helper's sheet: an action with a cost and no roll. On the lead's sheet: a state "assisted" switched on by hand or by handover, which shifts the pace override one step. The qualification check is advisory.
Gear gateA prerequisite reported by the action. The difficulty input gains a mod when the gate is not met. Nothing is blocked.
Gear effectProperties on the item, read by the outcome step through the character's equipped slots.
Gear bonus capA calculation on the rating that sums gear-origin mods and clamps at +2, with a description so describe shows the clamp.
Odds in wordsThe sheet app asks the engine for the chance that the roll property meets the difficulty at each pace, and turns the number into a word.
Flat diceAn ordinary roll input with a 1d10 or 1d100 expression and no margin step.

What the engine does not do yet

Each of these is small, genre-free, and would be a plan document of its own under the repository's working rules.

Why so little is needed

The rules were chosen to fit what the engine already does: sums of dice, one stored total, costs that return next turn, events the GM triggers. A system that counted successes, exploded dice or synchronised sheets would need a different engine. This one needs a probability function and the recurring-effects feature that was coming anyway.

Order of work

  1. The dice type gains "chance of at least N". Tests in the engine tests.
  2. The system assembly: skills as properties, the two pools, the task action with pace and margin, static defence, a reaction. Cyborgs stays as it is; this is a new system.
  3. A worked attack, a reaction and an aimed shot across two turns, as tests. The sniper scene is the test case.
  4. Recurring effects in the engine, then extended tasks in the system. The device and the archive are the test cases.
  5. Help, gates, gear effects and the bonus cap.
  6. Only then the skill list, the gear catalogue and the careers.
Open: the system's assembly name

The system has no name. Its assembly needs one before step 2. A placeholder that is obviously a placeholder is better than a name chosen in a hurry.
