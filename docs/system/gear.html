Gear

The three jobs of gear, the rating scale, the four progression currencies, what augmentation buys.

The three jobs of gear

Skill decides whether. Gear decides what, and how much.

Gear gates what can be attempted, sets the effect of success, and only rarely adds to the roll.

Three jobs
JobExamplesRule
GateA medkit to stabilise. A deck of sufficient rating to attack this node. Climbing kit to attempt the overhang. The right tool for the lock class.Without the gear the action is not available, or the difficulty rises one or two steps. The sheet reports this; it does not refuse.
EffectA weapon's damage. A medkit's heal. A deck's processing. A vehicle's speed. A sensor's range. A social implant's read.The quantity that margin improves. Better gear means a bigger quantity, and a bigger step when margin improves it.
BonusSmartlink. Quality tools. A good suit. Sensory enhancers.A small addition to the roll. Gear bonuses on one roll total +2 at most. Circumstance bonuses are separate and uncapped.
  • Most items do one job. The best do two. None do all three.
  • Gear that grants Tempo or Focus is a fourth kind, covered under augmentation below.
Why cap the bonus job

If gear mostly added to the roll, a rich novice would out-roll a poor expert and the certainty rule would blur. Capping roll bonuses keeps rating meaningful, and pushes the gear design towards gates and effects, where the interesting differences are.

Gear rating

  • Gear that gates has a rating on the same 0 to 8 scale as skills. A rating 4 deck can attack rating 4 nodes at the written difficulty; a rating 6 node is two steps harder for it.
  • Gear that sets effect has an effect value in the task's own units: damage rungs, stun pips, heal points, metres, range bands.
  • Gear that adds a bonus says +1 or +2 and to what.
  • Gear is described on the sheet by job, so a player reading an item sees "lets you: · makes it: · adds:" and nothing else.

Progression

Four currencies, each bought separately. A long campaign moves along all four.

What a character grows
CurrencyWhat it buysPace of growth
Skill ratingThe next step in a skill. Each step above 4 costs more than the last.Slow. A step or two per arc.
Specialisation+2 to a narrow use of a skill: pistols within a firearms skill, corporate systems within hacking, one language within linguistics. The +2 is not gear and does not count towards the gear cap.Moderate. The main way to feel sharper quickly.
TechniqueA new action. A double tap, a bypass that leaves no log, a cold read. Techniques do things ratings cannot, rather than making rolls better.Moderate. One per arc.
AccessThe right to buy or be fitted with gear: licences, contacts, clinics, cred. Money alone is not access.As fast as the fiction allows. The gear-heavy spine.
  • Tempo and Focus are not bought with skill points. A baseline of 3 may be raised to 4 once through training. Everything above that is augmentation.
  • Specialisations stack with gear bonuses but not with each other.
Why four currencies and a small rating scale

The certainty rule makes each rating step powerful: whole categories of task stop being rolls. If rating were the only progression it would have to be slow and the game would feel static. Specialisations, techniques and access give a character something new every few sessions without inflating the number that decides certainty.

Open: the cost curve

The exact cost of each rating step and of each specialisation and technique is not set. It depends on how experience is awarded, which belongs with the campaign rules.

Augmentation

  • Augmentation is gear installed in the body. It does any of the three jobs, and it is the main source of extra Tempo and Focus.
  • Wired reflexes and their kin raise Tempo. Decks, co-processors and attention implants raise Focus. Each grade raises its pool by one, to a ceiling of 8.
  • Restricted points come from gear that is specific to an action: a smartgun link, pursuit legs, a deck's attack co-processor.
  • Drugs and stimulants grant points for a few turns and charge for them afterwards. They are consumables that do the overdrive job.
  • What augmentation costs the character beyond money, if anything, is a setting decision and not a core mechanic. The rules above work with or without a humanity-style cost.
Why pools come from gear and not from levels

In the source genre, speed is bought. The samurai is fast because of what is in their spine; the decker is fast because of what is on their desk. Putting the pools in gear makes the fiction and the rules say the same thing, and it keeps the gear catalogue the place where a character grows.
