System 13 · Core mechanics

Damage

What a hit does. Fixed damage moved by margin, hit location chosen by margin, armour by location and damage type, Health and wounds, stun on Tempo, critical hits and fumbles.

The principle

One roll settles the hit. Margin decides where and how bad.

There is no damage roll and no location roll. The attack's margin band sets the damage step, who chooses where the hit lands, and whether it is a critical. Health is the fuse that says when a character goes down. Wounds are what the hit did to them, and they last.

  • The lethality target is gritty: two or three solid hits put an unarmoured person down. Armour matters a great deal, and so does what it does not cover.
  • Wounds cost pips, pace and capability. They never add a penalty to the dice.
  • The defender's sheet applies everything that reduces a hit: Soak, armour, cover. The attacker only says what arrived.
Why no damage roll

The attack roll already has a wide spread of outcomes, and its margin already moves the effect. A second roll would double the pending rolls per hit and blur the thing a player should feel, which is that a better shot hurts more. A fixed number also lets the sheet preview the result before anyone commits: "a strong hit puts him down".

Damage

Damage is a rung on a ladder

A weapon's damage is a number on the damage ladder. A strong hit moves it up one rung, an exceptional hit two. Anything that changes an effect "one step" moves one rung.

  • Melee and thrown weapons add steps from Body: Body 1–2 adds nothing, 3–5 adds one step, 6 or more adds two.
  • Every hit has a damage type. The type decides how armour works against it and what a critical does.

Why a ladder and not a dice expression

"One step" has to mean the same thing for a margin, a degraded part and a modification, or the mirrors break. Rungs make a step one word. They also keep the numbers small enough to say out loud. From 4 upwards each rung is roughly a fifth to a third bigger than the last, so one step is worth about the same at every size of weapon.

Hit location

The band says who chooses

A plain hit lands where the defender lets it. A good hit lands where the attacker wants it. Only an exceptional hit reaches the head.

Location by margin band
BandWho picks the locationDamageAlso
Success 0 to +4The defender: torso, an arm or a legWeapon's rungHeavy cover takes it first.
Strong +5 to +9The attacker: torso, an arm or a legOne rung upFound the gap. Cover does not help.
Exceptional +10 or moreThe attacker: any location, including the headTwo rungs upA critical.
  • Six locations: head, torso, left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg. They are spoken as words.
  • Head: damage one rung up. A head hit is never a plain hit; it is the attacker's choice.
  • Limbs: Health lost from one hit to an arm or a leg is capped at the wound threshold. The rest of the damage still counts towards the wound.
  • Called shot: declared before the roll. Each step of difficulty (+3) moves the choice of location up one band. A called head shot is +6. Damage and the critical still come from the real margin.
  • Heavy cover: a hit in the success band strikes the cover first. The cover's own armour comes off the damage before the defender's Soak and armour. A car door is 4, brick 10, an engine block 12. A strong or exceptional hit went round it.
  • The defender's sheet shows what each location would do before they pick: "torso · 2 · arm · 2 · leg · 6".
Why the defender picks on a plain hit

A narrow hit is a defender who nearly got out of the way and twisted so the round took the arm. Letting them choose puts that moment in their hands, uses armour coverage as a real decision, and needs no table. The attacker earns the choice with margin. A random location table would make the expert sniper and the panicked guard equally likely to hit a head. With margin, the head belongs to the shooter who earned it, or who paid for it with a called shot.

Why cap limb damage

A shot to the leg should cripple, not kill. The cap means limb hits make wounds without draining Health as fast as torso hits, so "shoot him in the leg" is a real tactical choice and not a worse way of killing someone.

Soak and armour

  • Damage taken = damage − Soak − armour at that location (after the damage type), never below 0.
  • Soak comes from Body and applies everywhere: Body 1–2 is 1, 3–5 is 2, 6 or more is 3. Implants can add Soak to a location.
  • Armour is one number per item, with the locations it covers and a kind: soft, hard or insulated. Worn pieces do not stack on the same location; the best one counts.
  • Armour wears. A hit that gets through armour ticks its wear, as for any item.

Why one armour number and a kind

A full resistance profile on every piece is eight numbers no one reads. Three kinds of armour against eight damage types gives the real cyberpunk trade-offs: the jacket that stops bullets and not knives, the plate that stops knives, the AP round that halves everything. The type also travels as one spoken word.

Health and wounds

Every hit takes Health. A big hit also leaves a wound.

Health is how much punishment a character absorbs before going down. A wound is a named injury at a location with an effect that lasts until it is treated.

  • Health = 10 + 2 × Body. Body 3 is 16; Body 5 is 20.
  • Wound threshold = Body + 4. Body 3 is 7.
  • Damage taken from one hit of at least the threshold is a Serious wound at that location. At least twice the threshold is Critical.
  • A critical hit raises the severity one tier: no wound becomes Serious, Serious becomes Critical.
  • A second Serious wound at the same location makes it Critical.

  • Bleeding is a state: −1 Health at the start of each of the character's turns until first aid stops it. It leaves a trail that Investigate can follow.
  • Each untreated Serious or Critical wound adds Acoustic +1 to signature, up to +2. Pain is loud.
  • Wound effects stack. Two Winded wounds on different hits cannot happen at one location, but an arm and a torso wound both count.
  • The sheet shows wounds as conditions in the ledger with their cost: WOUNDED · −1 TEMPO.
Why wounds cost pips and not dice

A −2 on every roll makes wounded characters fail more, which makes fights spiral and puts experts below the certainty line for reasons nobody can see. Losing a pip makes a wounded character slower, which the turn strip shows at a glance, and keeps the dice honest. "He is hit, he is slower" is also what the fiction says.

Why a Health pool as well as wounds

Wounds alone make light hits meaningless and every hit a table lookup. Health lets the stream of grazes add up to "down" without inventing a wound for each, and keeps the wound list short enough to matter.

Down, dying, dead

  • Down at Health 0 or below. A down character can talk, crawl and take Rushed actions costing 1 pip. Nothing else.
  • Dying when down with a Critical wound or with Health below 0. A dying character is Bleeding.
  • Dead when Health reaches minus the wound threshold. Body 3 dies at −7.
  • First aid that succeeds makes a dying character stable: still down, no longer bleeding.
  • Outside turn tracking, a dying character not stabilised by the next interval is dead.
Why the threshold is also the death line

It gives a down character a clock measured in turns that the whole table can see: a Body 3 character at 0 has seven turns of bleeding. That is long enough for the medic to reach them and short enough to make it the scene.

Stun

A stun takes pips, for a time

A stun is a number of pips and a duration. The pips are missing from the pool each time it refreshes, for as long as the duration lasts. Health is not touched. A stunned character is not hurt; they are slow.

  • A weapon or effect states its Stun in pips and its duration. Many weapons do damage and stun; some only stun.
  • Stun takes Tempo. A stun that hits the mind, such as a flashbang's disorientation or feedback through a datajack, takes Focus instead and says so. The rules are the same.
  • Margin moves stun as it moves damage: a strong hit adds 1 pip, an exceptional hit adds 2 pips and makes the duration one step longer.
  • Soak never reduces stun. Armour stops it only where it stops the type outright: insulated armour stops shock, a sealed suit or filter stops gas. A dart has to break the skin: armour of 2 or more at the location stops it.
  • Stuns add up. Each is its own entry in the ledger's running lane with its own end: TASER · −4 TEMPO · 2 TURNS LEFT.
  • Knocked out: when stunned pips reach the character's full pool, they are down until the longest stun ends. Down from stun is never dying.
  • A new stun interrupts a multi-turn action, as damage does.

Why stun takes pips

Tempo is time within a turn. A stunned character loses time, not accuracy, which matches how wounds work and keeps the dice honest. The sheet already shows missing pips, so a stun needs no new display, only an origin and an end.

Why a bigger pool resists stun

The same taser that drops a guard only slows the chromed samurai. That is the genre, and it gives security a reason to carry lethal weapons for the heavily augmented. The Shock critical burns implants, which is the answer to chrome.

Critical hits

A critical is an exceptional margin. Nothing else.

No natural rolls and no confirm rolls. An exceptional hit gets two rungs of damage, any location, one tier of severity, and the effect of its damage type.

Fumbles

A fumble is an attack's bad failure. The other side picks what goes wrong.

A margin of −5 or worse. The attacker's sheet shows the weapon's complications; the opponent chooses one. When a player fumbles, the GM picks. When an opponent fumbles against a player, the player picks.

  • A reaction can cause a fumble. A parry or dodge that leaves the attack at −5 or worse is how a riposte happens: the defender picks Overextended or Disarmed.
  • A task that cannot go badly cannot fumble. The certainty rule and Careful pace both take fumbles off the table, and the sheet says so.
Why the opponent picks

It mirrors the defender picking location on a plain hit: when the margin is near the line, the other side gets the choice. It removes the fumble table roll, and it gives the player on the receiving end of a bad attack something to do.

The exchange, spoken

  • The attacker says the band, the damage, the type and the location: "Hit, strong. Ten ballistic, left leg." Four slots on the defender's incoming-hit panel.
  • On a success band the location is "your pick". The defender's sheet shows the outcome for each location and the player taps one.
  • A critical is said as the band: "Critical. Twelve ballistic, head." The defender's sheet applies the type's effect.
  • A stun is a second short phrase: "Hit. One shock, torso. Stun four, three turns." The duration is spoken because the defender's sheet cannot know the weapon.
  • A fumble is said as "Fumble", and the attacker reads out the choices.
  • The defender's sheet holds Soak, armour coverage, cover and wounds, so it does all the reducing. The armour the GM spoke for the target panel is for the attacker's preview only.
Why the defender's sheet does the arithmetic

Only the defender's sheet knows which locations its armour covers, what kind it is and what is already wounded. Saying raw damage keeps the spoken sentence at four tokens and puts every reduction where describe can explain it.

Why recovery is extended tasks

The medic's job is a career, not a heal spell. Treatment takes days, gets safer with time, and is the scene where the medic is the one everyone waits for. A Critical wound left to a street doc is a real risk, and the chrome replacement is the genre's answer to it.

What it needs from the rest

  • Character: Health, wound threshold and Soak as derived scores, with their derivation shown.
  • Items: weapons carry a rung and a damage type. Armour carries a value, a kind and the locations it covers.
  • Sheet: an incoming-hit panel with four slots (band, damage, type, location), a per-location preview on a plain hit, and wounds as conditions in the ledger.
  • Engine: a mod carries a location tag, so armour and Soak can be summed per location. This is the same small tag the signature channels need. Bleeding and Alight are recurring effects, the feature already planned. A stun is a negative mod on a pool with a lifespan of one turn, several turns or until time passes, all of which the engine has. Wounds are states with an origin. The location and type slots are enumerated inputs.

Still open

Open: autofire and area

Whether a burst is one rung up for more ammunition, extra attacks from one roll, or a spray rule against several targets. Grenades assume one roll against every target in the area. Not decided.

Open: vehicles and drones

Whether vehicles take hits by location (engine, wheels, crew), or by Health plus condition like an item. Decide with the vehicle rules.

Open: the numbers

The ladder, the weapon rungs, Health of 10 + 2 × Body and a threshold of Body + 4 were chosen to meet the gritty target in the simulation above, not from play. The first fights will move them, and every table here is recomputed when they do.
