Pace

Time as the dial. The four paces, margin setting the time taken, extended tasks on the interval clock, and what failure costs.

The four paces

Time buys reliability, never brilliance

Every task is attempted at a pace. The pace sets how long the task takes and which dice are rolled. All four dice expressions have the same middle. Slower paces narrow the range; the slowest has no range at all.

  • Time is the task's base time multiplied by the pace. Base time is in Tempo or Focus points inside turn tracking and in intervals outside it.
  • The certainty rule uses the pace's lowest and highest totals. A Careful task is certain when rating + 6 meets the difficulty; a Methodical one when rating + 11 does.
  • Methodical is only available against a static difficulty. An opponent who can react, a target that is moving, a system that is actively defending: Careful is the slowest pace allowed.
  • Rushed is not available for tasks the skill marks as needing care. Nobody rushes brain surgery; they do it at Normal pace and accept the risk.
  • A task may fix its pace. A reaction such as a parry is always Normal. A background check run overnight is always Methodical.

Why the paces cross over

Below a need of 11 the slower paces win: they cut off the low rolls. Above it the faster paces win: they are the only way to reach the high rolls. So a desperate character facing a task beyond them is right to rush, and a competent one facing a task within reach is right to take their time. The table teaches that without anyone reading it; the sheet just shows the odds for each pace side by side.

Why aiming is not a bonus

Most systems give +2 for aiming. Here aiming is the Careful pace: it removes the miss by a mile and the fluke headshot alike. The same choice, the same dice and the same word apply to the surgeon and the safecracker, so there is one rule to implement and one for the sheet to explain.

Margin sets the time actually taken

Pace is the time committed before rolling. Margin says what it cost.

  • A refund in turn tracking is a one-turn bonus to the pool, so it can be spent on something else this turn.
Why time is the universal effect

In a heist, finishing early is as decisive as hitting hard. The decker who opens the door in one turn instead of three has changed the fight. Making time the thing every margin produces gives every career a shared language for "rolling well", on top of whatever the task itself improves.

Failure costs time

No free retries

A failed task spends its time. The same character may try again only at a slower pace than the one that failed. A task failed at Methodical pace is beyond this character.

  • This replaces "you may not retry" rules. Retrying is allowed; it just costs more time each attempt, and the clock is usually what the scene is about.
  • A different character may attempt the task at any pace.
  • A change in circumstances, new gear, a helper or a lower difficulty resets the ladder.

Extended tasks

A task long enough that the world moves while it is done: decrypting a drive over days, hacking a node while the guards close in, defusing a device under fire.

  • An extended task has a work total and a progress value. It completes when progress meets the total.
  • It is worked in intervals. Inside turn tracking the interval is the turn. Outside it the interval is a named time event the GM triggers: hour or day, as the task says.
  • Each interval the worker makes one task roll at their chosen pace. The margin gives progress.
  • Pace multiplies the interval: a Careful roll covers two intervals, a Methodical roll covers five. A Methodical worker rolls nothing and adds progress on a fixed schedule, if their rating is enough.
  • The task can be interrupted at any point. Progress is kept unless the task says it is lost.
  • Rushed is not available for extended tasks. The interval is already the smallest unit of time. Normal is the fastest pace.
  • An extended task has a tolerance: how many bad failures it absorbs before its consequence happens. A device might tolerate one slip; a search of an archive tolerates any number, because a false lead only costs time.

  • The sheet shows expected intervals to finish at each pace, from the distribution. For a work total of 6 on a Hard task, a professional sees "about 7 hours, could go badly" against "about 16 hours, safe" against "30 hours, certain".
Why intervals are GM-ticked events and not a clock

The engine's time outside turn tracking moves through events the GM triggers. Measured durations are out of its scope, and the fiction is fuzzy there anyway. Letting the GM say "an hour passes" and having the sheet queue one progress roll per worker matches how a table already runs downtime, and it keeps the sheet free-standing.

Who rolls, and when

  • A pace is chosen before the roll and cannot be changed after.
  • Pending rolls appear on the sheet as usual. A Methodical task never produces one.
  • A GM running many opponents sets their sheets to let the app roll. Nothing here changes that.
Open: the size of an interval

Hour and day are assumed. Whether a "minute" interval is needed for scenes between a fight and downtime, such as a tense search of a room, is not decided. If it is, it is one more named event and nothing else changes.
